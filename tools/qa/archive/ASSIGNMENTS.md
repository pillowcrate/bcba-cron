### day 67 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.2
taskDesc: "Identify and distinguish between stimulus and stimulus class"
Stimulus; stimulus class; the three kinds — formal (topography/physical features), temporal
(antecedent vs consequent), functional (common effect on behavior). Feature vs arbitrary classes.
Exam trap: calling a functional stimulus class a formal one.

### day 68 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.6
taskDesc: "Identify and distinguish between automatic and socially mediated contingencies"
Socially mediated vs automatic; automatic positive vs automatic negative reinforcement. Stress
that "automatic" names the ABSENCE of a social mediator — it is an inference, not a demonstrated
mechanism. Tie to why the alone/no-interaction FA condition is the discriminating evidence.

### day 69 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.10
taskDesc: "Identify and distinguish among concurrent, multiple, mixed, and chained schedules of reinforcement"
Separate all four on two crossed dimensions: is an SD present to signal the component, and are
components simultaneous or successive. Multiple vs mixed differ ONLY in the presence of a
discriminative stimulus. Chained differs from multiple in that reinforcement follows only the
terminal component. Include a question asking the reader to classify a described arrangement.

### day 70 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.11 · Operations and Processes
taskDesc: "Identify and distinguish between operant and respondent extinction as operations and processes"
NOTE the taskCode contains a middle dot qualifier — copy it exactly as written above.
Distinguish OPERATION (what is done) from PROCESS (the resulting behavior change), for operant
extinction (withhold the maintaining reinforcer) and respondent extinction (present CS without US).
Cover extinction burst, extinction-induced variability/aggression, spontaneous recovery, and that
the operation is function-specific (escape extinction vs sensory extinction vs withholding
attention). Exam trap: treating "extinction" as one procedure, or confusing it with response
blocking or punishment. Existing Day 16 covers applied extinction procedure — do not duplicate it;
stay on the operation/process distinction.

### day 71 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.15
taskDesc: "Identify examples of response maintenance"
Maintenance as an outcome distinct from acquisition and from generalization. Intermittent
schedules and resistance to extinction; behavioral trapping / natural communities of reinforcement;
why a follow-up probe, not end-of-treatment data, is the evidence.
Exam trap: conflating maintenance (over time) with generalization (across settings/stimuli).

### day 72 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.17
taskDesc: "Distinguish between motivating operations and stimulus control"
One of the most-tested distinctions. MOs alter the VALUE of a consequence and the momentary
frequency of behavior related to it (value-altering + behavior-altering). SDs alter the
AVAILABILITY of a consequence via differential reinforcement history. Cover Michael's distinction
between motivational and discriminative functions, the "was it ever unavailable in its absence?"
test, and CMO-T / CMO-R / CMO-S. Include a scenario forcing the MO-vs-SD call.

## g04 → out_g04.js
### day 73 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.20
taskDesc: "Identify the role of multiple control in verbal behavior"
Skinner (1957). Convergent multiple control (one response, several controlling variables) vs
divergent (one variable, several responses). Impure operants such as the mand-tact. Formal vs
thematic prompts. Why multiple control explains errors in speaker/listener training.
Exam trap: treating each verbal operant as under single control.

### day 74 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.21
taskDesc: "Identify examples of processes that promote emergent relations and generative performance"
Sidman. Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and the derived equivalence relation. Trained vs
derived relations; generative and recombinative responding. Distinguish an equivalence class from
a functional stimulus class. Include a question asking which property a described emergent
relation demonstrates. (Procedures for teaching these are day 102 — stay conceptual here.)

### day 75 · "Concepts and Principles" · B.22
taskDesc: "Identify ways behavioral momentum can be used to understand response persistence"
Nevin. The metaphor's two components: response RATE (velocity) and resistance to change (mass),
with mass a function of the stimulus-reinforcer relation / reinforcement rate in the context,
not the response-reinforcer relation. Applied translation: high-probability instruction sequence.
Counterintuitive exam point: richer reinforcement histories make problem behavior HARDER to
extinguish.

Imitation as a formally defined operant: point-to-point correspondence, formal similarity,

## g06 → out_g06.js
### day 79 · "Measurement" · C.2
taskDesc: "Distinguish among direct, indirect, and product measures of behavior"
Direct (observing behavior as it occurs), indirect (interviews, rating scales, checklists —
measuring reports ABOUT behavior), permanent product (measuring the effect left in the
environment). The two validity conditions for permanent product: the product must be produced only
by the target behavior, and the behavior must be the only way that product could arise. Real-time
vs post-session product recording. Exam trap: using permanent product when someone else could have
produced the product.

### day 80 · "Measurement" · C.7
taskDesc: "Measure efficiency (e.g., trials to criterion, cost-benefit analysis, training duration)"
Trials to criterion; sessions/duration to criterion; why percentage correct alone is not an
efficiency measure; cost-benefit and resource analysis. Efficiency is what adjudicates between two
procedures that are both effective (e.g. most-to-least vs least-to-most prompting).
REQUIRED: one question computing and comparing trials to criterion across two procedures, with
the arithmetic shown.

### day 81 · "Measurement" · C.12
taskDesc: "Select a measurement procedure to obtain representative procedural integrity data that accounts for relevant dimensions (e.g., accuracy, dosage) and environmental constraints"
Procedural integrity = measurement of the INDEPENDENT variable; IOA concerns the dependent
variable. That distinction is heavily tested. Dimensions: accuracy, dosage, exposure/duration,
adherence over time (integrity drift). Component checklist via direct observation vs self-report vs
permanent product. Why low integrity makes both positive and negative outcomes uninterpretable.
REQUIRED: one question computing an integrity percentage (steps correct / steps planned).

taskDesc: "Identify threats to internal validity (e.g., history, maturation)"
Name and define: history, maturation, testing/reactivity, instrumentation (incl. observer drift

### day 83 · "Experimental Design" · D.4
taskDesc: "Identify the defining features of single-case experimental designs (e.g., individuals serve as their own controls, repeated measures, prediction, verification, replication)"
The three-part logic — PREDICTION from a stable baseline, VERIFICATION of that prediction,

### day 84 · "Experimental Design" · D.5

sampling; the ethics of withholding treatment from a control group; where group designs are
genuinely superior (population-level questions, comparing established treatments at scale).
Exam trap: assuming group designs are simply weaker — they answer a different question.

### day 85 · "Experimental Design" · D.6
taskDesc: "Critique and interpret data from single-case experimental designs"
Within-condition: level, trend, variability. Between-condition: immediacy of effect, magnitude of
level change, degree of overlap, consistency across similar conditions. Baseline logic — why an
ascending baseline in the therapeutic direction precludes intervention, and why an unstable
baseline undermines prediction. Type I / Type II errors in visual analysis.
Include a question judging whether a described data pattern shows experimental control.

### day 86 · "Experimental Design" · D.8
taskDesc: "Identify rationales for conducting comparative, component, and parametric analyses"
Comparative (does A differ from B); component (which element of a package is necessary/sufficient —
drop-out vs add-in variants); parametric (what VALUE of a parameter works best — reinforcement
magnitude, prompt delay, DRO interval size). Multielement/alternating-treatments as the comparative
workhorse, with multiple-treatment interference as its limitation.
Include a scenario asking which of the three a described study conducted.

Tiers of risk: to clients (harm, ineffective or contraindicated treatment, lost trust); to the

resolution where appropriate, and the risk of failing to act.

### day 97 · "Behavior Assessment" · F.3
taskDesc: "Design and evaluate assessments of relevant skill strengths and areas of need"
Criterion-referenced vs norm-referenced assessment. The major behavior-analytic instruments and
what each is for: VB-MAPP (milestones, barriers, transition; built on Skinner's verbal operants),

### day 98 · "Behavior Assessment" · F.7
taskDesc: "Interpret assessment data to determine the need for behavior-analytic services and/or referral to others"
Two decisions: are behavior-analytic services warranted at all, and is referral to or coordination
with another discipline required. The medical rule-out obligation — pain, illness, medication
effects, sleep, seizure activity, sensory impairment — and why sudden-onset behavior change with no
environmental correlate is a referral, not an FA. Referral to SLP, OT, psychiatry, social work;
referral is not abandonment. Recommending against services when data do not support need.
Exam trap: proceeding to functional assessment when a medical cause should be ruled out first.

### day 99 · "Behavior-Change Procedures" · G.3
taskDesc: "Design and evaluate time-based reinforcement (e.g., fixed-time) schedules"
Noncontingent reinforcement / time-based delivery. FT vs VT schedules. Why NCR is an antecedent

of the response-reinforcer contingency. Dense initial schedules then thinning (constant-time,
response-dependent, fixed-lean). Principal risk: adventitious reinforcement when delivery coincides
with problem behavior, and how a brief postponement/omission contingency addresses it.

## g14 → out_g14.js
### day 103 · "Selecting and Implementing Interventions" · H.3
taskDesc: "Select socially valid alternative behavior to be established or increased when a target behavior is to be decreased"
The constructional approach — every reduction plan needs a replacement. Selection criteria:
functional equivalence (same reinforcer), plus the three response-efficiency dimensions from the
concurrent-operants literature — response effort, immediacy of reinforcement, rate/magnitude of
reinforcement — which determine whether the alternative actually outcompetes the problem behavior.
Recognisability to the natural community; whether the response is already in repertoire; the
dead-man's test. Exam trap: a topographically appealing alternative that takes more effort or pays
more slowly than the problem behavior will not be allocated to.

### day 104 · "Selecting and Implementing Interventions" · H.4
taskDesc: "Plan for and attempt to mitigate possible unwanted effects when using reinforcement, extinction, and punishment procedures"
Per procedure: reinforcement (satiation, dependence on contrived reinforcers, reinforcing an
unintended response class, ratio strain); extinction (burst, induced aggression, spontaneous
recovery, and accidental intermittent reinforcement making behavior MORE persistent); punishment
(elicited aggression, escape/avoidance of the punishing agent, modelling of aversive control,
response substitution, and over-use by staff because it is negatively reinforced for them).
Least-restrictive-alternative obligation. Exam trap: the accidental intermittent reinforcement of a
behavior nominally on extinction.

### day 105 · "Selecting and Implementing Interventions" · H.5
taskDesc: "Plan for and attempt to mitigate possible relapse of the target behavior"
Name and separate all three precisely — this trio is the entry: RESURGENCE (a previously reinforced
response returns when the currently reinforced alternative goes on extinction or is thinned);
RENEWAL (behavior returns when the context changes from the treatment context — ABA, ABC, AAB);
REINSTATEMENT (behavior returns after response-independent delivery of the maintaining reinforcer).
Also spontaneous recovery and how it differs. Mitigation: gradual thinning, treatment across
multiple contexts and people, maintaining some reinforcement for the alternative, boosters.
REQUIRED: a scenario question forcing the resurgence-vs-renewal-vs-reinstatement call.

### day 106 · "Selecting and Implementing Interventions" · H.6
taskDesc: "Make data-based decisions about procedural integrity"
The decision-making counterpart to day 81's measurement. The interpretive rule: a failed
intervention cannot be called ineffective until integrity is confirmed — the first response to flat
data is to check integrity, not change the plan. Integrity drift over time and across implementers.
Degraded-integrity effects differ by procedure: partially implemented extinction becomes
intermittent reinforcement and is worse than none. Choosing among retraining the implementer,
simplifying the procedure for contextual fit, or changing the procedure. Performance feedback and
BST to restore integrity. Include a scenario where the correct action is to restore integrity
rather than modify the intervention.

### day 108 · "Personnel Supervision" · I.3

### day 110 · "Personnel Supervision" · I.6
taskDesc: "Apply a function-based approach (e.g., performance diagnostics) to assess and improve supervisee behavior"
Staff performance problems have functions and the intervention must match the function. The

### day 111 · "Personnel Supervision" · I.7
taskDesc: "Make data-based decisions about the efficacy of supervisory practices"
The supervisor's obligation to evaluate their own practice, and the outcome measures that make it

Exam trap: evaluating supervision by hours logged or satisfaction alone rather than by performance
and client outcomes.

## g17 → out_g17.js
### day 112 · "Behavior-Change Procedures" · G.6
taskDesc: "Design and evaluate procedures to produce simple and conditional discriminations"
SCOPE: existing Day 19 already covers basic stimulus control, SD/S-delta and stimulus
generalization — do not re-teach it. Days 74 and 102 cover equivalence theory and equivalence-based
instruction — do not re-teach those either. Stay on the procedures.
Simple discrimination (one antecedent stimulus; differential responding in its presence vs absence)
vs conditional discrimination (correct response to a stimulus depends on a second conditional
stimulus — the four-term contingency). Match-to-sample as the standard conditional-discrimination
preparation: sample, comparison array, identity vs arbitrary/symbolic matching, simultaneous vs
delayed MTS. Discrimination training procedures: differential reinforcement across SD and S-delta,
errorless approaches (stimulus fading, stimulus shaping, superimposition). Stimulus salience.
The central failure mode: RESTRICTED STIMULUS CONTROL / overselectivity — the learner responds to
an irrelevant feature (position bias, a mark on a card, the therapist's gaze) rather than the
programmed dimension. Counterbalancing comparison positions, rotating exemplars, masked
presentation and probe trials detect it.
Exam trap to close on: high accuracy is NOT proof the intended discrimination was established;
restricted stimulus control produces correct responding for the wrong reason, and only a properly
designed probe distinguishes the two.
